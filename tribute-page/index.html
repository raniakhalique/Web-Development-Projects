A tribute to

Ada Lovelace

1815 – 1852 · Mathematician & Writer

She looked at a machine built to calculate and saw something no one else did: a machine that could create.

Who she was

Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, was a British mathematician raised on a strict diet of logic and science — her mother's deliberate antidote to the poetic temperament of her father, Lord Byron. That grounding gave her a rare gift: the ability to think about machines not just as calculators, but as instruments capable of working with anything that could be expressed in symbols.

In 1833, she met mathematician Charles Babbage and became fascinated by his design for the Analytical Engine, a mechanical general-purpose computer that would never be built in her lifetime.

What she did

Between 1842 and 1843, Ada translated an Italian paper on the Analytical Engine and appended her own notes — three times longer than the original text. Buried in Note G is what historians consider the first algorithm intended to be carried out by a machine: a method for computing Bernoulli numbers.

She went further than describing arithmetic. She proposed that such a machine could one day manipulate symbols, letters, and even music — an idea that would not be taken seriously again for over a century.
